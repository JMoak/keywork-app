    Main mode is the companion: the creature you last chose floats over your work, and the small team strip beside it picks another. Talk opens its conversation; Stead, or the M key, reveals the stead below to press things in. The panels work together. Press a workshop or a creature in the stead and the creature panel shows its summary and its ledger, questions first. Press a row in the ledger and that question or task opens in a short conversation of its own, beside the main one. Press the house for everything waiting on you. All of it runs in the page, on plain canvas. Lantern, Neon, Terrace and Deco are four themes, each a building style with its own colours. Switch the page between dark and light to see night and day. The drawing is a stand-in for scale, light and structure; the real art pass is the first step below. With the picture focused, the arrow keys turn it.

    What is off today

    • The creature is taller than its own workshop, so the buildings read as props.
    • The world is one grey ramp. Only the creature has colour, and there are three coats.
    • It is one strip. A town would only be a longer strip.
    • The newer branch adds painted stills in a second style: cream, olive, storybook. Its creature ideas are good (Folio, Tessera, Cairn, Purl, Hush, Knot). The paint is what clashes with the console.

    One place, built of blocks

    Flat at rest

    You look at one side, square on. It stays crisp pixel art on one grid, coloured from the theme, as the accepted art decision says.

    A quarter turn at a time

    The world has depth, and the next side comes round on a key. Depth hides things. Turning shows them.

    A plot for each creature

    Land is added when a creature is made. Nothing built ever moves or is taken away.

    Borrowed from FEZ: the flat sides, the quarter turn, and a map that turns. The look, the creatures and the land are Stead's own.

    Three sizes

    A theme is a style and its colours

    Every theme has a night and a day. All three now carry drawn light: pools under lamps and at lit doors, soft shadows, a glow in the sky, and haze with distance.

    A second architecture: Art Deco

    Deco is a whole way of building, not a recolour: jade stone, brass piers, amber windows, chevron cornices, stepped crowns and sunbursts. It is made of the same blocks and fills the same places as the other three, so everything the stead shows has somewhere to show.

    What every architecture supplies

    A style is a set of parts with fixed jobs. A new one is added by supplying these parts; the town, the roads, the growth and the signals do not change.

    Roads say who works together

    Every workshop also has a path to the porch, where work is brought to you. A creature walking a road with a crate is work being handed over.

    Today the world is not told who works with whom. The engine carries messages between creatures; counting them for each pair is new work.

    Each creature its own

    The six creatures at world size, generated with Codex as tiny sprites and put on the grid. The kid is the earlier hand-made stand-in.

    Up close

    Six creatures, twelve poses each, from Codex, put on the grid by the same script and playing here, each strip in a different colour. Each moves and works in its own way.

    Each creature from eight directions, turning in place: the four sides and four corners the world can be seen from. Until now a creature was one flat drawing that always faced the same way.

    Three distances. In the world a creature is a small figure. Step into its workshop and it is drawn large and fine, with all its poses. And it can float beside your work as a companion, as the companion window does today.

    Earned, never lost

    Better tools as it progresses: the same creature at work with a wooden, an iron, a gold and a star-metal hammer. Last, wearing a medal.

    • A creature earns from what it has really done: a hundred conversations, a first night worked through, ten hand-offs with one partner, a project that became a tower, a year standing.
    • Its tools improve with its range: the more kinds of real tool it has used well, the better the hammer it is drawn with.
    • You earn for the place: the first creature, a hamlet, a town, the first paved road, the first tower. A stone on the green becomes a gold statue.
    • A run can earn too. Seven days in a row with work delivered earns a ribbon. When the run ends, the ribbon stays and nothing is said.
    • Each one is a fact with its proof, listed where firsts are remembered today, and none is ever taken away.

    Project directories become buildings

    Each directory a creature works in is a small building on its own plot, in its colour, lit while a thread runs there. Pressing one opens that folder.

    Nothing is scanned for this. Every conversation already records its directory, so the count is free. A repository states its number of tracked files in the first twelve bytes of its index. A folder that is not a repository grows only by the files the creature has touched.

    Four sides of a workshop, four things to open

    Pressing a side would open that pane, by you or by the creature, through the window tools already built.

    The map

    Alive, cheaply

    • A lit window is a thread running. Smoke is a tool in use. A lit loft is a child thread.
    • Sails turn while a model is loaded. Flags move. The sky follows the clock, as it does now.
    • It is drawn only when something changes: a few frames a second at rest, the full rate only during a turn, and nothing when hidden or when you ask for stillness.

    What stays as it is: indexed pixel art on one grid, the world as a pure reading of events, nothing decorative that is not informative, and growth that never decays.

    Order of work

    1. The look, still. One stead from the front in the app's real renderer, day and night, and a contact sheet of figures. You judge it before anything is built on it.
    2. The block world. The same stead as blocks with four sides and the turn.
    3. Growth. Plots, the hamlet, the town, and the camera's whole steps.
    4. Roads. The engine counts who works with whom; the world lays roads from it.
    5. Life. Walkers, lamps, smoke, sails, inside the drawing budget.
    6. The map. Machines as linked land.
    7. The roster. Each creature as a figure and a portrait, in batches, with sheets for you.

    Steps 1 and 7 are taste and stay with you and me, on sheets like this one. Steps 2 to 6 are mechanics, for Opus 5.5 agents in worktrees of their own.
